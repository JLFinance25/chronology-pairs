## Predictions, one by one

**P1 (Qwen3 8B: right-both-ways rises with the gap, and flips are most common at 1 to 2 years). Mostly supported.**
- Right both ways rises in every band: 18%, 23%, 25%, 29%, 37%, 68% (gaps 1, 2, 3 to 5, 6 to 10, 11 to 25, 26+).
- Flips are highest at 1 year (66%) and 2 years (63%), then 60%, 56%, 53% and 29%. But the ranges for 1 to 25 years
  overlap (for example 1 year: 49% to 82%; 3 to 5 years: 49% to 70%). The only clear drop is at 26+ years.
- P1 names Qwen3 8B only. Llama 3.1 8B, on the same measure (no prediction was written for it), barely moves:
  right both ways goes from 19% to 29%, and flips stay between 59% and 62% at every gap.

**P2 (Qwen3 14B beats Qwen3 8B on right-both-ways in every gap band). Supported.**
- 14B is higher in all six bands: 36% vs 18% (1 year), 48% vs 23% (2), 53% vs 25% (3 to 5), 56% vs 29% (6 to 10),
  68% vs 37% (11 to 25), 86% vs 68% (26+).
- PREREG names no test for P2. Each model's own ranges overlap at 1 year (14B: 23% to 50%; 8B: 8% to 32%) and barely
  overlap at 2 years (35% to 62% vs 11% to 36%). From 3 years up they do not overlap.
- Worth knowing: 14B flips much less (31% at 1 year, 9% at 26+), but it is also *wrong both ways* more often on close
  pairs (33% at 1 year vs 15% for 8B). The bigger model is more decided, not only more right.
- Even 14B does not reach a date resolution: at 26+ years it is right both ways on 86% (needs 95%) and flips on 9% (needs at most 5%).

**P3 (among all-stable triples, circles are rarer than the shuffle baseline). Supported for all three setups.**
- Qwen3 8B: 14 circular triples out of 66,393 all-stable triples. The shuffles averaged 3,281 (95% of shuffles: 3,012 to 3,602).
  None of the 1,000 shuffles was that far from the shuffle average, so p < 0.001.
- Llama 3.1 8B: 30 out of 27,761, against a shuffle average of 6,015 (5,829 to 6,199), p < 0.001.
- Qwen3 14B: 140 out of 188,198, against a shuffle average of 12,424 (11,931 to 12,961), p < 0.001.
- In plain English: when a model gives the same answer both ways, those answers almost never go in circles. Its
  steady answers fit one timeline, even where that timeline is wrong.

**P4 (thinking off: the type C share grows as lists get longer).**
- Qwen3 8B: **supported.** 19% (14% to 24%) at 5, 25% (21% to 30%) at 10, 31% (25% to 38%) at 20. The 5 and 20 ranges do not overlap.
- Llama 3.1 8B: **not supported.** 16% (9% to 23%), 20% (16% to 24%), 18% (14% to 24%). Flat, with overlapping ranges.
- Qwen3 14B: **partly supported.** 35% (28% to 41%) at 5, 47% (42% to 52%) at 10, 47% (42% to 52%) at 20. Length 20
  is above length 5, with ranges that just miss overlapping (the reading PREREG's clarification uses for "rises").
  But all of the rise is between 5 and 10. From 10 to 20 it is flat.

**P5 (integrity failures grow with list length, for every setup). Supported for all three setups.**
- Lists with an event left out, repeated or invented: Qwen3 8B 0%, 12%, 75% at lengths 5, 10, 20; Llama 3.1 8B 0%, 6%, 72%.
- They fail in different ways (counted from `data/processed/results/<setup>_lists.json`). Of Qwen3 8B's 45 broken
  20-event lists, all 45 left something out, 40 repeated an event, and 6 invented a code. It also kept numbering
  past 20 in 87% of 20-event lists. Of Llama's 43 broken 20-event lists, all 43 left something out (79 events in
  all), only 5 repeated one and 2 invented one, and it never kept writing past the end.
- Qwen3 14B: 0%, 6%, 50%. Of its 30 broken 20-event lists, all 30 left something out (49 events in all), 6 repeated
  one and 4 invented one. It kept writing past 20 in 13% of 20-event lists.

**P6 (thinking on lowers type C more than type B).** Not tested yet: `qwen3-8b-think` has not run.

## Q2: where list mistakes come from (PREREG section 6 decision rules)

- **Qwen3 8B: "a real long-list penalty."** The rule fires through the penalty test. At length 20 the model made
  0.80 more wrong neighbor pairs per list than its own pair answers predict, with a range of 0.33 to 1.28, above zero.
  The type C test did not fire: type C rose (19% to 31%) but stayed below 50%. At lengths 5 and 10 the penalty
  was about zero. So the extra list-only trouble shows up only at 20 events, and it is small: under one extra
  mistake per list. **Read with care:** this verdict uses the section 5 reading of cut-off replies, which Jayden confirmed after seeing that the other reading gives "in between" (flag 2).
- **Llama 3.1 8B: "in between."** Type C was at most 20% at every length (16%, 19.9%, 18%), which is half of the
  "mostly in the pairs" rule (19.9% at length 10 only just passes). The other half failed, but in the opposite direction from a penalty.
  At lengths 5 and 10 the penalty's range sits **below** zero (-0.32, range -0.53 to -0.09; and -0.42, range -0.69
  to -0.15). Its lists had *fewer* wrong neighbors than its pair answers predict. At length 20 the range is wide
  (-1.63 to 1.18). By the letter of the rules that is "in between". In plain English: no sign of extra list-only
  mistakes for Llama. Its lists did slightly better than its own pair answers would suggest.
- **Qwen3 14B: "in between."** Neither rule fires. Type C is well above 20% at every length (35%, 47%, 47%), so the
  verdict is not "mostly in the pairs". For "a real long-list penalty", type C did rise from length 5 to 20, but it
  reached 46.7% at 20, just under the 50% line (its range runs to 52%). The length-20 penalty's range includes zero
  (+0.13, -0.64 to 0.86). Worth knowing: at length 10 the penalty is clearly above zero (+0.42, 0.13 to 0.70), but
  the rule looks only at length 20. Of the three setups, 14B has by far the largest share of list-only mistakes,
  and its verdict sits close to a line. The cut-off question (flag 2) does not affect it: none of its valid lists hit the limit.

## Things to flag

1. **Llama's list repeats were not all identical.** At temperature 0, all 500 repeated pair questions gave the same
   answer, but only 24 of 30 repeated lists did (Qwen3 8B: 30 of 30). The 6 that changed were all 20-event lists,
   6 of the 11 twenty-event repeats. In all 6 the scored order changed, and one went from valid to broken (counted
   directly from `data/raw/model_runs/main/llama3.1-8b.jsonl`). The analysis scores the first answer, as planned.
   What we know: long Llama list answers on the PC are not fully repeatable. Why: not known. A guess is that the
   PC's graphics card does arithmetic in a slightly different order from run to run (UNVERIFIED). Either way,
   Llama's 20-event results carry extra noise that the ranges do not include.
2. **Cut-off replies: PREREG reads two ways, and the Qwen3 8B verdict depends on it. Decided: section 5.** Section 3 says "a reply
   cut off at the limit counts as unreadable". Section 5 says a list that keeps writing past N "doesn't count against it".
   `src/analyze.py` (committed before any main data was looked at) follows section 5. 16 Qwen3 8B lists wrote all
   their events and then ran into the 400-token limit, including 9 of its 15 valid 20-event lists. **Exploratory
   check** (`src/explore_cutoff.py`): if those 16 count as unreadable, Qwen3 8B's verdict becomes "in between".
   Its length-20 penalty is then +0.50 with a range of 0.00 to 1.00, resting on 6 lists. Qwen3 14B and Llama had none,
   so their verdicts do not change. **Decision (Jayden, Oct 12, 2026, in chat):** use the section 5 reading and keep `src/analyze.py` as it is.
   That decision came *after* we knew it changes Qwen3 8B's verdict. It is defensible, because the code was committed
   before any main data was looked at and section 5 is the more specific rule for lists. But any write-up must say
   so and report the section 3 result next to it.
3. **Few valid 20-event lists.** The length-20 list results rest on 15 lists (Qwen3 8B), 30 (Qwen3 14B) and 17 (Llama).
   As PREREG section 6 says, the list ranges may be too narrow because lists share events.
4. **A reproducibility fix in `src/analyze.py`.** The circular-triple shuffles looped over the gap bands in an order
   that Python changes from run to run, so the pre-registered seed (42) did not give the same shuffles twice.
   It now loops in sorted order, and two runs with different Python hash settings give identical files. This
   changed only Qwen3 8B's shuffle baseline: average 3,277.95 to 3,280.88, range 2,989 to 3,588 to 3,012 to 3,602.
   The observed 14, the p-value and every other number are unchanged. No rule changed.
5. Llama ran on the PC and the Qwen3 models on the Mac (PREREG Deviations), so a Llama vs Qwen3 difference could
   partly be a machine difference. Same Ollama version and settings.

## Exploratory observations (not in PREREG; ideas, not results)

- **Llama hardly uses what it knows on pair questions.** It named the exact year for 97 of 120 events. But on
  single pair questions with events more than 25 years apart it was right only 58% of the time (`per_question_accuracy`
  in its results file), close to the 50% of guessing, and it answered "A" 79% of the time. A guess: the habit of
  picking the first option drowns out its knowledge of the dates.
- **Why Llama's penalty is negative: a guess.** If its pair answers mostly reflect the "A" habit, then ordering
  events from them is close to random, so the "predicted" mistakes are high and its real lists beat them.
  Not tested.
- **The B/U/C mix partly mirrors how common each pair label is.** For Qwen3 8B, 53% to 66% of all pairs 1 to 25 years
  apart flip (`pairs_by_gap.csv`), and 56% to 61% of its list mistakes are type U. A fair test of whether list
  mistakes land on "unsure" pairs more than chance would compare each type's share with how common that label is
  among all pairs inside the same lists. That comparison is not in PREREG and has not been run.
- **Across models, the type C share tracks how often the model is right on close pairs.** Right both ways on pairs
  1 to 10 years apart vs type C share of list mistakes (both from the tables): Qwen3 8B 18% to 29% vs 19% to 31%;
  Llama 19% to 24% vs 16% to 20%; Qwen3 14B 36% to 56% vs 35% to 47%. So a higher type C share may mostly mean a
  model that gets more pairs right, not one that is worse at lists. That matters when reading P4 and the type C
  half of the Q2 rules across models. (1 to 10 years because list neighbors are 1 to 10 years apart by design;
  actual wrong neighbor pairs can be further apart.) P6 compares Qwen3 8B with itself against the same pair answers, so it is less exposed.
